# Guiding Principles

**Status:** v0.4 working draft

These principles hold in any size organization and with any technology.

---

## 1. Two things can be true

A term can legitimately mean different things to different parts of the business. Product gross margin and service gross margin are both correct. The failure is not the difference; it's the difference staying **invisible behind a shared label**.

Authoritative does not mean globally singular. Represent each legitimate meaning as a **perspective** with its own owner, instead of forcing false consensus.

## 2. Context selects the perspective. When context can't, ask

People and AI should use the perspective that fits the situation: a product review, a services review, a board deck. When the situation doesn't settle it, the right answer is a question ("which gross margin?"), never a guess.

## 3. Meaning is separate from measurement

A concept says what something means. A Metric Contract says how a number about it is calculated. Keep them linked, never collapsed.

## 4. Every meaning has an owner

Someone accountable decides what a term means within their scope. Central governance handles cross-functional conflicts and enterprise defaults. Owners closest to the work decide the rest.

## 5. Similar formulas can hide different meanings

Two metrics can share a formula and still measure different things, because the inputs mean different things. Compare meanings, not just calculations.

## 6. Say what can and can't be compared

If two perspectives can't be meaningfully compared or combined, say so explicitly. Averaging product and service margin looks reasonable and usually isn't.

## 7. Meaning changes; history shouldn't break

Definitions change with reorganizations, acquisitions and regulation. Version them, date them, name replacements, and keep past numbers interpretable.

## 8. Trust, but check

Good definitions don't guarantee correct use. Check that reports and AI tools use the intended meaning, and be able to back up consequential numbers.

## 9. Govern in proportion

Not every term needs governance. Start where confusion, reuse, AI use, regulatory exposure or executive visibility make it matter.

## 10. Work with what exists

SMF is technology-independent. Catalogs, semantic layers, metric stores, knowledge graphs and governance platforms are ways to implement it. Other frameworks are partners, not competitors.

## 11. Adopt in pieces

Every practice can be adopted on its own, at any size, and combined as needs grow. No practice requires another, a fixed sequence or a central system.
