# The Lifecycle of a Meaning

Every discipline manages its object across a lifecycle: services in ITIL, data in DAMA, models in model risk management. SMF manages **meaning** across this one:

```text
Define → Establish authority → Resolve in context → Measure → Implement
      → Exchange → Consume → Assure → Evolve ─┐
   ▲                                          │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Stage | What happens to the meaning | Gross margin example | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Define** | The concept and its perspectives are written down | Gross Margin; product, service and consolidated perspectives | Discovery, Modeling |
| **Establish authority** | Owners are named for each perspective; conflicts are decided or declared | Services Lead owns service margin; Controller owns consolidated | Semantic Authority |
| **Resolve in context** | Rules say which perspective applies where, and when to ask | Board → consolidated; leadership meeting → ask | Semantic Resolution |
| **Measure** | Each perspective gets a Metric Contract | Service GM excludes product sales, cost is labor-based | Measurement Management |
| **Implement** | Contracts are built into reports, semantic layers, spreadsheets | Service GM in the services KPI workbook | Semantic Implementation |
| **Exchange** | Meaning moves between systems without losing its perspective | Export to a new BI platform | Semantic Interchange |
| **Consume** | People and AI use the meaning | An AI assistant answers a margin question | Semantic Resolution |
| **Assure** | Checks confirm the right perspective was used; key numbers are backed up | The assistant must ask in a leadership setting | Semantic Assurance |
| **Evolve** | Definitions change with the business; history stays readable | Freight moves into product cost from next fiscal year | Lifecycle Management |

## How the lifecycle and the practices relate

- The **lifecycle** describes what happens to a meaning over time.
- The **practices** are how an organization takes care of those stages.
- Organizations can adopt one practice, several or all of them. A small business might only formalize *Measure* and *Resolve*. An enterprise might run all nine.
- The lifecycle is not a waterfall. Most organizations enter partway, usually when two numbers disagree.
